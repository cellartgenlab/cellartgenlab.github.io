Pharmaceutical Capsule Manufacturing

Capsules are widely used pharmaceutical dosage forms designed to contain powders, granules, pellets and other suitable formulations within a dissolvable shell. Their convenient administration, flexible formulation options and suitability for different product requirements make them an important part of modern pharmaceutical manufacturing.

Each project is assessed according to its intended application and applicable regulatory requirements.

CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY

Types of Pharmaceutical Capsules

Hard Gelatin Capsules

Hard gelatin capsules generally consist of two parts: a body and a cap. They are commonly used for suitable powder, granule and pellet formulations. Selection depends on compatibility, moisture sensitivity and product specifications.

HPMC Capsules

Hydroxypropyl methylcellulose capsules are plant-derived shell options used in suitable pharmaceutical and nutraceutical applications. Their selection requires evaluation of shell properties and formulation compatibility.

Powder-Filled Capsules

Designed for suitable powdered formulations. Important considerations include powder flow, blend uniformity, fill weight and moisture control.

Granule-Filled Capsules

Granules may be selected where particle engineering and formulation design support the required filling, handling and product performance.

Pellet-Filled Capsules

Multiple pellets can be filled into a capsule shell. Depending on the formulation, pellets may be designed for specific release characteristics.

Specialty Capsule Formulations

Certain products require specialized shell, fill-material or release characteristics. These projects need product-specific development and appropriate evaluation.

Capsule Formulation Development

Capsule formulation development begins with understanding the active ingredient, intended dosage form, target fill weight, material characteristics and product performance requirements.

Active Ingredient Evaluation

Assessment of the active ingredient's physical and chemical properties, compatibility and handling requirements.

Excipient Selection

Selection of suitable diluents, binders, glidants, lubricants and other excipients based on formulation requirements and compatibility.

Blend Development

Evaluation of powder characteristics, mixing behaviour and blend uniformity to support consistent capsule filling.

Fill Weight Development

Establishing suitable filling parameters based on capsule size, bulk density, formulation properties and product specifications.

Raw Materials and Excipients

The selection and control of raw materials play an important role in capsule manufacturing. Each material must be assessed against its approved specifications and intended use.

  • Active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs)
  • Hard gelatin or HPMC capsule shells
  • Diluents and fillers
  • Binders and disintegrants
  • Glidants and lubricants
  • Other suitable formulation excipients
PROCESS OVERVIEW

Capsule Manufacturing Process

The manufacturing sequence is established according to the approved formulation, process design and product specifications. A typical powder-filled capsule workflow may include the following stages:

01

Material Receipt

Receipt, identification, quarantine and approved release of materials.

02

Dispensing and Weighing

Controlled dispensing of approved ingredients according to batch documents.

03

Sifting and Screening

Preparation of materials using suitable screening procedures where required.

04

Blending

Mixing of formulation ingredients using defined process parameters.

05

Capsule Filling

Filling the approved formulation into compatible capsule shells.

06

Inspection and Sorting

Inspection for relevant defects and removal of non-conforming units.

07

Quality Evaluation

Testing and review against the applicable product specifications.

08

Packaging and Release

Packaging, labelling and final batch review before authorized release.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are pharmaceutical capsules?

Capsules are dosage forms in which a suitable formulation is enclosed within a shell, commonly gelatin or HPMC.

What is the difference between gelatin and HPMC capsules?

They differ in shell composition and material properties. Selection depends on formulation compatibility, product requirements and intended application.

Can capsules contain powders and pellets?

Yes. Suitable capsule shells can be filled with powders, granules or pellets, depending on formulation design and process suitability.

Which tests are important for capsules?

Relevant tests may include appearance, fill weight, assay, content uniformity, disintegration, dissolution and microbiological evaluation, as applicable.

How is capsule packaging selected?

Packaging is selected based on product stability, moisture sensitivity, storage conditions and distribution requirements.
