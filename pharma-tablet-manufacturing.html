Understanding Pharmaceutical Tablet Manufacturing

Tablets are among the most widely used pharmaceutical dosage forms. They are solid preparations containing one or more active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), together with suitable excipients. Tablet development requires a systematic understanding of ingredient characteristics, formulation composition, manufacturing processes, product performance and packaging requirements. The objective of pharmaceutical tablet development is to establish a formulation and process capable of consistently meeting its predefined quality specifications and intended performance.

Tablet Formulation

Selection and evaluation of APIs and excipients according to product specifications, compatibility and dosage form requirements.

Granulation Technology

Evaluation of wet granulation, dry granulation and direct compression approaches based on material characteristics.

Compression Technology

Understanding compression parameters, powder flow, blend uniformity and physical tablet characteristics.

Coating Technology

Exploration of suitable film coating and functional coating approaches based on product objectives.

Quality Evaluation

Product-specific testing and evaluation against established specifications and applicable quality requirements.

Packaging Solutions

Consideration of packaging compatibility, moisture protection, stability and product presentation.

DOSAGE FORM KNOWLEDGE

Types of Pharmaceutical Tablets

Tablet dosage forms can be designed to achieve different release, administration and product performance objectives. The appropriate design depends on the active ingredient, intended use, formulation characteristics and relevant regulatory requirements.

  • Immediate-release tablets
  • Film-coated tablets
  • Enteric-coated tablets
  • Modified-release tablets
  • Extended-release tablets
  • Chewable tablets
  • Dispersible tablets
  • Effervescent tablets
  • Orally disintegrating tablets
  • Bilayer tablets
01 / TABLET DOSAGE FORMS

Immediate-Release Tablets

Immediate-release tablets are designed to release their active pharmaceutical ingredient without an intentionally prolonged release mechanism. Their formulation may involve suitable diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants and other excipients. Formulation development considers disintegration behaviour, dissolution characteristics, API properties and the intended product specifications. The selection of excipients and processing methods must be supported by appropriate development studies.

02 / TABLET DOSAGE FORMS

Film-Coated Tablets

Film coating involves applying a thin polymer-based layer to the tablet surface. Depending on the formulation, coating may help improve product appearance, swallowing characteristics, handling and protection against selected environmental factors. Coating development considers polymer selection, coating composition, process parameters, drying conditions and finished-product evaluation. The coating approach must be appropriate for the specific product.

03 / TABLET DOSAGE FORMS

Enteric-Coated Tablets

Enteric-coated tablets use specialised coating systems designed to resist release under specified acidic conditions and allow release under appropriate intestinal conditions. Their development requires careful consideration of polymer properties, coating integrity, dissolution performance and product stability. Product-specific testing is essential to establish whether the intended release characteristics are achieved.

04 / TABLET DOSAGE FORMS

Sustained and Modified-Release Tablets

Modified-release tablets are developed to alter the rate, location or timing of active ingredient release. These systems may involve polymer matrices, specialised excipients or other formulation technologies. Development requires evaluation of material compatibility, release profiles, formulation robustness, dissolution behaviour and stability. The design must be supported by suitable pharmaceutical development and product performance studies.

05 / TABLET DOSAGE FORMS

Chewable and Dispersible Tablets

Chewable tablets are intended to be chewed before swallowing, whereas dispersible tablets are designed to disperse in a suitable liquid before administration. Both dosage forms require formulation approaches that consider taste, texture, disintegration, dispersion behaviour and product stability. Excipient selection and product evaluation play an important role in achieving suitable physical and performance characteristics.

FORMULATION SCIENCE

Pharmaceutical Tablet Formulation Development

Formulation development is a critical part of pharmaceutical product development. It involves the systematic evaluation of the active ingredient, excipient functionality, processing requirements and intended product performance. A well-designed formulation should be suitable for the intended dosage form and capable of meeting predefined quality specifications.

API Characterisation

Evaluation of physical and chemical characteristics of the active ingredient, including solubility, particle properties and stability considerations.

Excipient Selection

Selection of suitable excipients based on their functionality, compatibility and contribution to formulation performance.

Compatibility Studies

Assessment of potential interactions between active ingredients and formulation components using suitable scientific methods.

Powder Flow Evaluation

Understanding flow behaviour and physical properties that may influence blending, filling and compression.

Stability Considerations

Evaluation of product stability under appropriate study conditions and predefined specifications.

Process Optimisation

Assessment of formulation and process variables to support reproducible product quality.

INGREDIENT SCIENCE

Raw Materials and Pharmaceutical Excipients

Raw materials are fundamental to pharmaceutical tablet development. The active pharmaceutical ingredient provides the intended pharmacological activity, while excipients perform supporting functions within the formulation. Common excipient categories include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants, glidants, coating materials, colours and flavouring agents where appropriate. Selection should consider functionality, compatibility, quality, supplier qualification, material specifications and applicable pharmaceutical standards. Every material must be suitable for its intended use and controlled through an appropriate quality system.

MANUFACTURING TECHNOLOGY

Granulation Technologies in Tablet Manufacturing

Granulation is used in many tablet manufacturing processes to improve powder handling and processing characteristics. The selection of a granulation method depends on the formulation, material properties, moisture sensitivity and intended manufacturing process. Wet granulation: Uses a suitable liquid binder system to form granules, followed by appropriate drying and sizing steps. Dry granulation: Uses compaction techniques to form granules without the addition of a granulating liquid. Direct compression: Uses suitable powder blends that can be processed directly into tablets when their material and flow properties permit.

TABLET PRODUCTION

Tablet Compression Process

Tablet compression transforms a prepared powder blend or granulated material into a defined solid dosage form. The process involves consideration of tooling, compression force, feed behaviour, tablet dimensions and physical characteristics. Important development parameters may include tablet weight, thickness, hardness, friability, appearance and disintegration performance. Appropriate process monitoring and documentation help establish reproducibility and support consistent product quality.

ADVANCED PROCESSING

Tablet Coating Technology

Tablet coating can serve aesthetic, functional and protective purposes. The coating system may be selected according to the intended product characteristics and release requirements. Film coating, enteric coating and other specialised coating approaches require appropriate formulation selection and process evaluation. Relevant parameters include coating composition, application conditions, drying, appearance, adhesion and finished-product performance.

QUALITY ASSURANCE & QUALITY CONTROL

Quality Control in Pharmaceutical Tablet Manufacturing

Quality control plays an important role throughout tablet development and manufacturing. Product-specific testing is used to assess whether materials, processes and finished products meet established requirements.

Weight Variation

Assessment of individual tablet weights according to applicable specifications.

Hardness

Evaluation of tablet mechanical strength using suitable test methods.

Friability

Assessment of tablet resistance to abrasion and mechanical handling.

Disintegration

Evaluation of the time required for tablets to break down under specified test conditions.

Dissolution

Evaluation of drug release behaviour using an appropriate dissolution method.

Assay & Uniformity

Assessment of active ingredient content and dosage unit uniformity as applicable.

PRODUCT PERFORMANCE

Stability Studies and Product Shelf Life

Stability studies help evaluate how product quality may change over time under defined environmental conditions. They are important for understanding the effects of temperature, humidity, light and packaging on pharmaceutical tablets. A suitable stability programme is designed according to the product, market requirements and applicable regulatory guidance. Study results support shelf-life assessment, storage recommendations and packaging selection.

PACKAGING SOLUTIONS

Pharmaceutical Tablet Packaging

Packaging is an important part of pharmaceutical product protection and presentation. The selected system should be compatible with the tablet formulation and help maintain product quality throughout storage and distribution. Common packaging considerations include blister packaging, strip packaging and suitable bottle systems. Material selection may consider moisture protection, light sensitivity, product stability, transport conditions, labelling and regulatory requirements.

DOCUMENTATION & COMPLIANCE

Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Documentation

Pharmaceutical manufacturing requires appropriate documentation and quality systems. Documentation supports traceability, process control, review and accountability throughout product development and production. Depending on the product and applicable requirements, records may include master manufacturing instructions, batch manufacturing records, material specifications, analytical records, equipment records, deviations, change controls and stability documentation. Manufacturing and quality activities must be performed in accordance with the applicable licensing, regulatory and GMP requirements.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Tablet Manufacturing FAQs

What is pharmaceutical tablet manufacturing?

It is the process of developing and producing solid pharmaceutical dosage forms containing active ingredients and suitable excipients under defined quality requirements.

What are the main stages of tablet manufacturing?

Common stages include material assessment, weighing, blending, granulation where required, compression, coating where applicable, testing and packaging.

What is the difference between wet and dry granulation?

Wet granulation uses a suitable liquid binder system, while dry granulation uses compaction without adding a granulating liquid.

Why is tablet coating performed?

Coating may improve appearance, provide a functional barrier, support product protection or achieve a specific release objective depending on the coating system.

Which quality tests are relevant to tablets?

Depending on the product, tests may include weight variation, hardness, friability, disintegration, dissolution, assay and content uniformity.
